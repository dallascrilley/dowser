# Archaeological Methodology

## Phase 1: Temporal Excavation

Begin by mapping the temporal dimensions of concepts, creating a structured excavation record that documents when ideas emerged, peaked, declined, and their current status. Assess archaeological significance and preservation priority.

**Key Activities:**
- Map when concepts first appeared
- Document peak influence periods
- Track decline patterns
- Assess current status
- Determine preservation priority

## Phase 2: Lineage Mapping

Trace the genealogy of concepts, identifying ancestors, siblings, descendants, and mutations. Document cross-pollination from other fields and the evolutionary pressures that drove changes.

**Key Activities:**
- Identify concept ancestors
- Map sibling concepts (contemporary alternatives)
- Track descendants (evolved forms)
- Document mutations and adaptations
- Identify cross-field pollination
- Analyze evolutionary pressures

## Phase 3: Paradigm Shift Analysis

Analyze major transitions in thinking, documenting what was lost, what survived, and what fled to other fields. Preserve archaeological remains and assess potential recovery value.

**Key Activities:**
- Identify pre-paradigm shift thinking
- Document the transition period
- Catalog what was lost in the shift
- Identify what survived the shift
- Track ideas that migrated to other fields
- Preserve archaeological remains
- Assess recovery value

## Phase 4: Decay Analysis

Examine how and why concepts decay, identifying decay types, rates, and drivers. Determine what artifacts remain useful and under what conditions revival might occur.

**Decay Types:**
- Obsolescence (replaced by better solutions)
- Contextual decay (still valid elsewhere)
- Half-life decay (gradual loss of relevance)
- Catastrophic decay (sudden obsolescence)

**Key Activities:**
- Identify decay type and rate
- Determine decay drivers
- Assess remaining utility
- Identify revival conditions

## Phase 5: Revival Archaeology

Identify and analyze revived concepts, understanding their original era, dormancy period, revival triggers, and modifications. Assess hybrid vigor from revival and predict future cycles.

**Key Activities:**
- Identify revived concepts
- Document original era
- Measure dormancy period
- Identify revival triggers
- Catalog modifications upon revival
- Assess hybrid vigor
- Predict future revival cycles
