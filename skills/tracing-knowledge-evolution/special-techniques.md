# Special Techniques

## Intellectual Carbon Dating

Determine the age of ideas through various markers:

**Dating Markers:**
- **Language Patterns**: Terminology and phrasing used
- **Citations**: What sources are referenced
- **Assumed Knowledge Base**: What's taken as given
- **Technical Limitations Mentioned**: What constraints are discussed
- **Contemporary Concerns Addressed**: What problems are being solved

## Conceptual DNA Analysis

Trace genetic markers in ideas:

**Genetic Markers:**
- **Core Unchanging Elements**: The DNA that persists
- **Mutation Points**: Where ideas diverge
- **Recombination Events**: Where ideas merge
- **Horizontal Transfer**: Ideas from other fields
- **Epigenetic Modifications**: Context-dependent changes

## Knowledge Geology

Understand forces shaping idea landscapes:

**Geological Forces:**
- **Tectonic Shifts**: Paradigm changes (sudden, dramatic)
- **Erosion**: Gradual decay over time
- **Sedimentation**: Knowledge accumulation (layering)
- **Volcanic Events**: Revolutionary ideas (explosive emergence)
- **Glaciation**: Periods of stagnation (frozen development)

## Extinction Event Catalog

Document knowledge die-offs:

**Extinction Types:**
- **Mass Extinctions**: Broad paradigm shifts that kill many ideas at once
- **Background Extinctions**: Normal obsolescence rate
- **Living Fossils**: Old ideas still in use unchanged
- **Lazarus Taxa**: Ideas that disappear and return
- **Elvis Taxa**: Ideas falsely reported as alive (zombie concepts)

**Cataloging Process:**
1. Identify extinction event
2. Classify type
3. Determine scope and impact
4. Document casualties
5. Identify survivors
6. Predict future patterns
